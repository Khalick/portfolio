A place to feel, not perform

Almost every app on your phone is built for an audience. I wanted to build one that has none.

Open any social app and notice what it asks of you. Post, and see who reacts. Share, and count the likes. Even when you’re talking about something painful, there’s an audience watching and a number keeping score.

That design is brilliant for engagement. It is terrible for honesty. People don’t write what they feel when they know they’re being watched. They write what will land well.

SoulScript started from a simple question: what would a digital space look like if it were built only for the person using it?

Private by default

In SoulScript every entry is private unless you choose otherwise, one entry at a time. Your journal sits in a vault you lock with a PIN or your fingerprint. There are no followers, no likes and no feed. Nobody is keeping score, because there is nobody else there.

Privacy here isn’t a setting buried in a menu. It’s the foundation the rest is built on. The data model isolates every user’s entries at the database level, and you can export or delete everything you’ve written at any time.

People don’t only feel in words

Most journaling tools assume you want to write. Many people don’t. Some days the most honest record of how you feel is a voice note you couldn’t have typed, a photo of where you were, or thirty seconds of video. So SoulScript lets you mix text, photos, video and voice in a single entry.

Each entry starts with a quick emotional check-in: pick from sixteen moods and set how intense it feels, from one to ten. It takes seconds, and it’s often the hardest part. Naming a feeling is the first step to understanding it.

Seeing the pattern

One entry is a moment. A year of entries is a map. SoulScript’s emotional timeline colour-codes your days so you can see patterns you’d never notice from inside them: the hard weeks, the breakthroughs, the quiet stretches in between. “On this day” brings back what you were feeling a year ago, which is often a reminder of how far you’ve come.

What it isn’t

SoulScript is not therapy and doesn’t pretend to be. It’s a safe place to put what you’re carrying. For hard moments it offers thoughtful prompts and breathing exercises, and it points to real crisis resources when someone needs more than an app can give.

Why it matters to me

Lokari is about helping businesses be seen. SoulScript is almost the opposite: a place where you don’t have to be seen at all. Underneath, both come from the same belief. Technology should be built around how people really live, not around what keeps them scrolling.

Your feelings are valid. You deserve a place to put them that belongs only to you.
